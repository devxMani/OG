One of my favorite quotes is by Charles Bukowski:

> "If you're going to try, go all the way. Otherwise, don't even start… this could mean losing girlfriends, wives, relatives and maybe even your mind. It could mean not eating for three or four days. It could mean freezing on a park bench. It could mean jail. It could mean derision. It could mean mockery, isolation. Isolation is the gift. All the others are a test of your endurance, of how much you really want to do it. And you'll do it, despite rejection and the worst odds. And it will be better than anything else you can imagine… you'll be alone with the gods, and the nights will flame with fire. You will ride life straight to perfect laughter. It's the only good fight there is."

This quote perfectly captures how I feel about effort and ambition. Over the past few years, I've failed a lot. I've been told no more times than I can count. And yeah, it sucks. It's one of the worst feelings there is. But I also believe we only get one shot at life. We only have the time and energy to truly go after a handful of things. So when you do decide to chase something, I think you owe it to yourself to go all the way.

That doesn't mean failure isn't allowed. It totally is. But there's a difference between failing when you gave it everything you had, and failing when you know you held back. The first one hurts less. Because at least then, you know it was out of your hands.

## On Building & Creation

I believe the best ideas emerge at the intersection of curiosity, constraint, and persistence. There's something magical about taking two unrelated concepts and weaving them together to create something entirely new. Whether it's code, design, writing, or any other craft—the most meaningful work happens when you stop optimizing for perfection and start optimizing for understanding.

Building is thinking. Every line of code, every design decision, every word written forces you to clarify what you actually believe. The act of creation is the act of learning about yourself.

## On Time & Attention

We live in an age of infinite content and infinite options. Our attention has become the most precious commodity. I try to be intentional about where I direct it. This means saying no often. It means consuming deeply rather than broadly. It means surrounding myself with people and ideas that challenge me to think differently.

Quality over quantity isn't just a design principle—it's a life principle. The things that matter most are usually the things that took time.

## On Growth & Discomfort

Growth lives in discomfort. When you feel like you don't know what you're doing, when you're building something that scares you, when you're exploring ideas that challenge your existing worldview—that's where the real learning happens. I've learned more from projects that terrified me than from projects that felt safe.

This doesn't mean you should be reckless. It means you should be brave enough to try things that matter.
